# Which one worked? One company, two ways (v10)

Built 2026-09-11 from Tom's structure on the 10 September call and Evan's correction on the 11th.
A choose your own adventure case study, four rounds, one company per round shown two ways.
Prototype, draft for Tom.

## The rule that shapes every round

**One company, two ways.** Evan rejected a draft round that set Apple's "1984" against Progressive's
Flo: "one is a way bigger company and it's spent way more on it, and the series loses to that."
He was right. Comparing two companies compares two budgets, so it cannot prove anything about the
approach. Putting one company's own two approaches side by side holds the budget, the brand and the
audience still, and the approach is the only thing left changing.

It also removes any need to criticise the losing side, which was Tom's rule: our prospects are
probably making the thing on the losing side right now, so the page says there is another way that
can work better, never that the first way was foolish.

## The four rounds

Tom's order from the call. Each rung teaches one thing, and they build.

| Round | The lesson | The company, two ways | What settles it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Repetition | Rico's Tacos, twice a week, against a single Tide film | 200,363 views on one episode against 26,840 on the film, both P&G |
| 2 | Format and theme, not just repeating | The Best Worst Team against twenty years of "Should've gone to Specsavers" | 23.3M views, 5 minutes average watch, brand consideration up 35 percent, The Drum, 13 Nov 2024 |
| 3 | Audience over product | The Stratos jump against a Gives You Wiiings cartoon | 49,046,929 views against 251,520 on the same Red Bull channel, and 8 million watching the jump live |
| 4 | Own it, do not rent it | Second Act against the MailKimp read on Serial | Mailchimp moved the money even though the sponsorship beat every other sponsor on the show |

Round 2 is the one that makes the ladder work. The Specsavers line repeated for twenty years, so
it passes round 1's test and still loses round 2. Repeating is not the same as returning.

Round 4 is deliberately framed as a win the company walked away from. The sponsorship lifted
mentions 81 percent around the Serial premiere, ahead of Audible at 19 percent, and Mailchimp still
moved the budget into its own studio because the audience its shows brought in looked like its most
valuable customers.

The gate refuses the build if the rungs are out of Tom's order, if any side has no film, if a
non-official upload does not disclose itself, if a round names no measure or no dated source, if a
round has anything other than exactly one correct why answer, if the scoring copy does not match the
round count, or if either phrase Evan cut ever reappears.

## Rejected, and why

- **Apple "1984" against Progressive's Flo.** Two companies, two budgets. Evan's objection, and it was correct.
- **Dove "Real Beauty Sketches".** The official upload returns a 403 from oEmbed, so it will not embed at all. Only fan copies resolve, at a fraction of the campaign's real reach.
- **YETI.** Its cooler launch film out-views every YETI Presents documentary, which is the wrong way round for round 3.
- **GEICO's Gecko.** No official upload exists anywhere, and GEICO's growth cannot be credited to the Gecko because several campaigns ran at once inside one large increase in spend.
- **Volvo Trucks.** Its highest-view film is itself the product demonstration.
- **The YETI 30 percent fishing sales figure** and **the Red Bull 7 percent sales figure**. Neither traces to a named publication that could be read.
